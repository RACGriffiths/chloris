# Decision log

Each design decision, why it was made and what else was considered. **Agreed** means settled. **Proposed** means recommended but not yet confirmed by the maintainer.

To change a decision, update its row and the [spec](spec.md) in the same commit.

| # | Decision | Choice | Why | Alternatives considered | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D01 | Build or adopt | Build a new app | HortusFox, the closest self-hosted option, felt dated and clunky in use. Plant-it is now an Android-only app with no server. Hosted apps (Planta, Greg) keep your data and charge subscriptions. | HortusFox, Plant-it, Planta, Greg | Agreed |
| D02 | Name | Chloris; repository `chloris` | The Greek nymph of flowers. Short, easy to spell, and no large repository uses it. | hesperides (long, easy to misspell, similar names exist), aegle, ladon, pomona | Agreed |
| D03 | Licence | AGPL-3.0-or-later (SPDX `AGPL-3.0-or-later`) | Copyleft: anyone can use, change and self-host it, but anyone who distributes it, or runs a modified version for other people over a network, must share their source under the same licence. Selling it isn't forbidden, but nobody can sell or host a closed-source version. The app shows a link to its source so forks have an obvious place to point theirs. | MIT (the original choice: simplest, but allows closed-source commercial forks); GPL-3.0 (doesn't cover running it as a hosted service); a non-commercial licence such as PolyForm Noncommercial (forbids selling, but isn't open source) | Agreed |
| D04 | Hosting | A self-hosted Node service (Proxmox LXC), with a Docker image too | Free to host, no provider lock-in, ready to open-source. | Cloudflare Workers with D1 and R2: tied to one provider, and free-tier CPU limits are tight | Agreed |
| D05 | Internet access | Left to whoever hosts it. The app needs `ORIGIN` and the proxy header settings. | Works behind Caddy, a Cloudflare Tunnel, or both. | Building in a specific tunnel or VPN | Agreed |
| D06 | Accounts | Chloris's own login system, separate from other projects | A standalone project; a problem in one can't affect another. | Sharing an auth service with other projects | Agreed |
| D07 | Login | Passkeys, with magic links as fallback; no passwords | Nothing to leak, reuse or reset. | Passwords with 2FA; OAuth providers (ties login to third parties) | Agreed |
| D08 | Database | SQLite only, in WAL mode, via Drizzle | One file, trivial backups; usage is expected to stay light. | Supporting Postgres as well: more moving parts, not needed at household scale | Agreed |
| D09 | Photo storage | A configurable folder, separate from the database's. Files are write-once, deletion is delayed, and backups run database first. | Photos can live on a mounted drive, and backups stay consistent even when taken separately. | Storing photos in the database (bloats it and its backups); S3-compatible storage (possible later behind a storage interface) | Agreed |
| D10 | Email | Any SMTP server, set in config; optional | Provider-agnostic: nearly every email provider offers SMTP. | A specific provider's API (Resend, Postmark): ties hosts to one provider | Agreed |
| D11 | Reminder channels | Web push first, email as fallback | Reaches the phone without a native app. | Native apps; SMS | Agreed |
| D12 | Sitter permissions | Sitters can log care and add photos and measurements, but not edit plants | They're the ones looking at the plants while you're away. | View and log only | Agreed |
| D13 | "Checked, not needed" | Records a check and moves the due date on by a recheck delay, 2 days by default, set per plant | Watering on a fixed interval ignores how dry the soil actually is. | Snooze only | Agreed |
| D14 | Tenancy | Multi-home data model from day one; one home in the UI at first | Adding tenancy later means reworking every query and permission check. | Single home now, retrofit later | Agreed |
| D15 | Access control | Fixed roles per home (owner, carer, viewer, sitter); code checks permissions through `can()`, never role names | Easy to reason about and test; custom roles can come later without touching the checks. | Per-user custom permissions (hard to reason about, easy to misconfigure); BetterAuth dynamic roles (possible later) | Agreed |
| D16 | Frontend | SvelteKit (TypeScript) with the Node adapter | Least ceremony for a maintainer who isn't a frontend specialist; one codebase for pages and API. | Next.js (heavier, leans towards Vercel hosting); React Router or TanStack Start (more assembly); Nuxt | Agreed |
| D17 | Reminder style | One daily digest per person per home, with quiet hours | One useful message a day instead of a stream of per-plant pings. | A notification per plant per task | Proposed |
| D18 | HEIC photos | Rejected on upload with a message asking for a JPEG | sharp's standard build can't read HEIC. iPhones normally convert to JPEG when uploading from a web page, and the browser's resize produces JPEG anyway. | A libvips build with HEIC support in the Docker image (bigger image, patent-encumbered codec, and plain Node installs miss out); a WebAssembly HEIC decoder (another dependency) | Agreed |
| D19 | Invitations | A single-use, expiring link the inviter copies and shares, in version 1; sending it by email when email is on | Lets a second person join a home without SMTP, which is optional. | Email-only invites (no way to join without SMTP); adding members from the command line | Agreed |
| D20 | First due date | Until a task is first logged, its schedule's creation date stands in for the last event | Keeps the due date derived from stored data, with no separate "first due" field to keep in sync. | Due immediately on creation (floods the Today view when adding plants); asking for a last-done date (an extra step when adding plants) | Agreed |
| D21 | Component kit | shadcn-svelte, which is built on Bits UI | Ready-styled, accessible components that are copied into the repository and can be edited like the app's own code. Bits UI on its own has no styling, so every control would have to be designed by hand. | Bits UI on its own | Agreed |
| D22 | SQLite driver | better-sqlite3 | Mature and widely used, and supported by Drizzle and drizzle-kit. Its online `backup()` makes a consistent copy while the app is running, which covers both the backup before migrations and the backup command. | Node's built-in `node:sqlite` (no native module, but newer and less proven with Drizzle); libSQL (a fork of SQLite aimed at Turso's hosted service, whose extras Chloris doesn't need) | Agreed |
| D23 | Package manager | npm | Comes with Node, so the Dockerfile, CI and an LXC install need nothing extra. Node 25 and later no longer include corepack, the tool that set up pnpm and Yarn automatically. | pnpm (faster and stricter about undeclared dependencies, but one more tool to install everywhere); Yarn | Agreed |
| D24 | Dependency updates | Dependabot, with minor and patch updates grouped into one pull request a week | Built into GitHub, with no app to install. Grouping keeps the number of pull requests manageable for one maintainer. | Renovate (more flexible grouping and automerging, but a third-party app with more configuration) | Agreed |
| D25 | Repository visibility | Private for now, to be made public later | Kept private while the foundations are built. While it is private, GitHub's free plan can't require CI to pass before merging, and Actions minutes are limited, so CI should stay light. | Public from the start | Agreed |
| D26 | Account creation | No open sign-up. Accounts come from the command-line tool's sign-in link or from an invite link. Only an account the command-line tool created, or later allowed, can create a home; an account that joined by invite never can, and without a home it is told it has no access. | A household server facing the internet shouldn't let strangers make accounts or homes. Whoever runs the server decides who can start a home, and invited people only ever join one. | Open sign-up by magic link, each new account getting its own home; letting any signed-in account without a home create one; letting only the very first account create a home | Agreed |
| D27 | BetterAuth's HTTP endpoints | The browser can reach only the passkey endpoints. Sign-in links, sessions and passkey management go through the app's own form actions, which call BetterAuth on the server; homes and memberships go through the data-access layer and `can()`. | BetterAuth's organisation endpoints check roles their own way and know nothing of membership expiry, and its other account endpoints aren't wanted. One path for every change keeps `can()` the only authority and keeps forms working without JavaScript. | Exposing BetterAuth's whole API and relying on its own checks | Agreed |
| D28 | Invite links | Chloris's own `invite` table. An invite carries a role (owner, carer or viewer in version 1), works once and lasts 7 days. The link holds a random token, and only its hash is stored, so the inviter sees the link once, when it is made. Opening it without an account asks for a name and email address, then creates the account, its membership and a session together. Someone signed in joins with their own account, even if they already belong to another home. | BetterAuth's invitations are tied to one email address, which D19's copyable link isn't, and accepting one runs BetterAuth's own role checks rather than `can()` (D27). With only a hash stored, a copy of the database can't be used to join. A week leaves time to get round to a link sent by message. Sitters wait for version 2, when an invite can carry an end date. There is no screen for changing roles yet, so an owner invite is how a home gets a second owner. BetterAuth's empty `invitation` table stays only because its organisation plugin won't start without it. | BetterAuth's invitations, called from the server; storing the token so the link can be copied again later; 24 or 48 hours; carer and viewer only; refusing to join a second home while the UI shows one | Agreed |
| D29 | Plant fields in version 1 | Every field in the spec's Plant row except the cover photo, including the status (with its date and note) and the parent plant, which have no screens yet. Tags are a list of lower-case labels in a JSON column on the plant. | The roadmap wants version 2 to add screens rather than migrations, so columns whose screens come later go in now. The cover photo has to point at a photo, and the photo table arrives with the photo pipeline, itself a version 1 item, so it waits for that. Tags on the plant row are scoped by the plant's own `home_id` with nothing more to check, and a household has few enough plants for SQLite's `json_each` to filter them quickly. | Only the fields this item's screens use, with each later item adding its own migration; a cover photo column now, without its foreign key; a separate tag table | Agreed |
| D30 | Rooms on screen, and deleting | Plants are listed under their rooms, with a filter for one room. A Rooms page adds, changes and deletes rooms. Only an empty room can be deleted. Plants aren't deleted in version 1: the Memorial view takes those that died, were given away or were sold. A plant's page shows sections for care, photos and measurements before they exist, saying what each will hold. | Every plant is in a room, so deleting a room with plants would have to move or delete them; refusing loses nothing and keeps the rule simple. Deleting a plant has to deal with its care history and its photo files, which have a grace period, so it waits until those exist. The empty sections show where the page is going. | Rooms as the top level, each listing its plants; managing rooms only from the plant form; deleting a room moving its plants to one chosen at the time; deleting plants now; a plant page with only the details | Agreed |
| D31 | When a task is due | Due dates are calendar dates in the home's time zone, never stored (D20). A check counts only if it was made since the task was last done. Doing or checking a task ends its snooze, and undoing that care doesn't bring the snooze back. A snooze runs for a day or three from today, or until a chosen date after today and within a year, and is offered only once a task is due. | Plants are watered on days, not at times, and the home's time zone decides when a day starts, as it will for digests. Without the "since" rule, an old check with a long recheck delay could hide a task that has since been done and fallen due again. Without ending the snooze, a long one (set before going away) would hide the next due date after a sitter has done the task. Snoozing a task that isn't due would change nothing, so it isn't offered. | Due times rather than dates; counting every check and snooze as the spec's "latest of" reads literally; storing when a snooze was set and comparing it with the log, so undoing care restores the snooze (one more column for a rare case) | Proposed |
| D32 | Undoing care | Only the person who logged care can undo it, at any time, while they have `care:log`. Right after logging there's an Undo button; from the plant's history it takes two taps. Undoing deletes the event, and the due date goes back by itself. | Undo is for mistakes, and the person who made one is the one who knows. Letting a sitter delete the owner's history isn't wanted, and with no audit log yet a deletion leaves no trace, so it stays with the author. Deleting rather than hiding keeps every due date simply derived from the log. | Anyone with `care:log` removing any event; owners and carers removing anyone's; undo only for a few minutes after logging; a `deleted_at` column | Proposed |
| D33 | Care fields in version 1 | The schedule stores a winter interval now, used when winter intervals arrive in version 2, as D29 did for plants. A care event's photo waits for the photo table, like the cover photo. The recheck delay is set on each schedule, as the data model puts it, so it is set per plant (D13) and per task. | Version 2 should add screens rather than migrations. A photo column needs the photo table to point at. A plant may want a shorter recheck for misting than for watering. | Leaving the winter interval for version 2's migration; one recheck delay per plant | Proposed |
| D34 | The Today view | Tasks due today or overdue on active plants, by room and then by plant, both in name order, each with Done and "Not needed". A room offers "Water all" (or the like) for a task due on two or more of its plants: it logs the task on the plants the page listed whose task is still due when tapped, says which it left alone, and one Undo removes the batch. A task's own buttons here also skip a task that's no longer due. "Done today" lists everyone's care, yours included, with Remove on your own. Snooze stays on the plant's page. | A page left open, or two people working at once, would otherwise log a plant twice, which "without double watering" rules out; acting only on the plants shown means a tap never logs care on a plant nobody saw. The plant's page keeps logging unconditionally, because there Done also records care given early. Listing your own care too shows the household's whole day in one place, and gives a mistaken tap a fix after the Undo message has gone. A snooze needs a choice of how long, which would crowd a list built for one tap. | "Water all" logging whatever in the room is due when tapped; a bulk action for the whole home; logging even when the task is no longer due; listing only other people's care, as the spec first read; a Snooze button on each task | Proposed |
| D35 | Photo files | Three JPEGs per photo: a 400-pixel square thumbnail, a medium size up to 1280 pixels and a full size up to 2560 on the longest side, never enlarged. sharp re-encodes each (mozjpeg, quality 80) in sRGB with no metadata. Once JavaScript has loaded, the browser sends a 2560-pixel JPEG rather than the original. Uploads must be JPEG, PNG or WebP, judged by their first bytes, up to 20 MB and 100 megapixels. Files are named `PHOTOS_DIR/<home id>/<file key>-<size>.jpg`, the file key being a random UUID kept apart from the photo's id. Each is created exclusively and flushed to disk before the database row is written. They are served at `/photos/<id>/<size>` to members with `home:view`, cached as private and immutable; a missing file is answered with a placeholder. | 2560 pixels shows a leaf's detail on any phone or laptop and keeps a photo near 1 MB, and the original never leaves the phone. JPEG opens anywhere, which suits export and plain files on disk. Square thumbnails make a tidy grid and fit the plant tiles. A folder per home keeps its photos together for export and deletion. A random key means a photo's address says nothing about its files. Judging the type by its first bytes keeps SVG, TIFF and the rest away from sharp's decoders. Files never change, so browsers can keep them. | WebP or AVIF (smaller, but not every program opens them, and AVIF is slow to encode); keeping the original at full resolution (several MB each, and once JavaScript has loaded it never reaches the server); thumbnails in the photo's own shape; one folder for every home; naming files by the photo's id; no caching | Proposed |
| D36 | When a photo was taken | The camera's date: EXIF's date taken, or failing that its date digitised, read with the offset from UTC the camera recorded, or in the home's time zone if it recorded none. The browser reads it before resizing and sends it with the photo; without JavaScript the server reads it from the original. A date in the file itself wins over one sent with it. With no date, a malformed one, one before 1990 or one still to come, the photo is dated when it was added. The date a photo was last changed is ignored. | Most phones have recorded the offset since about 2017; without one, a photo of a houseplant was most likely taken at home. Editing software sets the last-changed date, which would date an edited photo by its edit. A photo with no date was most likely just taken, so the time it was added is a fair guess. | The file's last-modified time (on a phone, usually when it was chosen); asking for a date with every photo (an extra step; correcting a date can come with the timeline); the server's time zone | Proposed |
| D37 | Photo folder and upload size at start-up | The default photo folder, inside `DATA_DIR`, is made if missing. A `PHOTOS_DIR` that is set must already exist and be writable, or Chloris refuses to start. A `BODY_SIZE_LIMIT` too small for a 20 MB photo logs a warning at start-up, and an upload over it gets a message naming the setting. | `PHOTOS_DIR` is for a mounted drive. If the drive isn't mounted, making the folder would put photos on the local disk under the mount point, hidden once the drive is back and missing from its backups. Refusing to start shows the problem at once. The Node adapter's 512K default is too small even for a resized photo, but it's the adapter's setting, so Chloris warns rather than refuses. | Making `PHOTOS_DIR` whatever it is; failing only when a photo is uploaded; refusing to start on a small `BODY_SIZE_LIMIT` | Proposed |
| D38 | Photos before the timeline | The photo table comes with the plant's cover photo column (D29) and the care event's photo column (D33), whose screens come with the photo timeline. A plant's page lists its photos as square thumbnails, the latest taken first, each with its date and opening the full size, and "Add a photo" takes a photo and an optional caption. Deleting photos, viewing them in the app and choosing a cover come with the timeline; removing a deleted photo's files after the grace period comes with the scheduler. | Both columns were only waiting for a table to point at, and one migration for the photo data leaves the timeline item to screens. A plain grid shows uploads working without designing the timeline twice. | Leaving both columns to the timeline item; no photos on the page until the timeline | Proposed |
| D39 | The photo timeline and each photo's page | A plant's page shows its photos under a heading for each month they were taken in, the latest first, three square thumbnails to a row, each with its day. The latest 12 are shown, and "Show all" lists the rest. Each opens a page of its own with the medium size (tapping it opens the full size), when it was taken, who added it, the care it was added with, and Newer and Older links. The caption and the date it was taken can be corrected there: a time left as it was keeps the seconds the field doesn't show, and a time still to come or before 1990 is refused. The compare view and the camera overlay stay in version 2, as the roadmap has them. | Month headings make a long run of photos easy to scan, and show how often a plant was photographed. Latest first matches the care history and shows the plant as it is now. Twelve keeps the plant's page short. A page for each photo works without JavaScript, can be linked to, and has room for its actions. Correcting the date was left for this item in D36: a photo with no date of its own is dated when it was added, which can be wrong. | Oldest first, as the story of the plant's growth; a full-screen viewer over the plant's page with swiping (needs JavaScript, and has no room for the actions without it); every photo on the plant's page; a separate timeline page | Proposed |
| D40 | Changing and deleting photos | Changing a photo's caption or date, or deleting it, needs `photo:add`, and only the person who added it may, unless they also have `plant:edit` (owners and carers), who may change any of the home's photos. Deleting takes two taps and only marks the photo deleted, as the spec's Photos and backups section describes. A deleted cover goes back to the latest photo. | A sitter who adds a blurred photo can remove it, but not the household's photos. Owners and carers keep the plants' records, and someone has to be able to remove the photos of a member who has left. During the grace period a mistaken deletion can still be put right on the server, since the row and files are kept. | Only the person who added it, as with care (D32), which would leave a departed member's photos for good; anyone with `photo:add`, which would let a sitter delete the household's photos; a new `photo:delete` permission, which changes the roles table (D15) | Proposed |
| D41 | Photos with care | Log other care takes an optional photo, offered to those with `photo:add` as well as `care:log`. The photo and the care are stored together or not at all. The photo joins the plant's timeline like any other; its page names the care, and the care history shows its thumbnail. A photo with no date of its own is dated when the care was done. Undoing the care keeps the photo, and deleting the photo keeps the care. The Today view's one-tap buttons take no photo. | A repot or a pest treatment is when a photo is most worth taking, and one form saves a second upload. Care logged for yesterday most likely had its photo taken then too. Undo is for mistakes in the log (D32), while the photo is still a photo of the plant, and deleting it is a step of its own, so neither takes the other with it. | Adding the photo separately and linking it to the care afterwards; undoing care deleting its photo too; a photo on the Today view's buttons, an extra step on the quickest screen | Proposed |
| D42 | Cover photos | A plant's tile shows the photo chosen as its cover, or else its latest photo, or with no photos its initial. Choosing one needs `plant:edit` and is done from the photo's page, which can also go back to the latest. Tiles use the 400-pixel square thumbnail, on the Plants list, the Today view and the plant's page. | The latest photo shows a plant as it is now with nothing to do, so most plants never need a cover chosen. Choosing one is for when the latest is a close-up or a bad day. It changes how the plant looks to everyone, so it goes with editing the plant. | Only a chosen cover, with the initial until then; the first photo becoming the cover; anyone with `photo:add` choosing it | Proposed |
| D43 | Memorialising and restoring a plant | A plant's Edit page ends with "Died, given away or sold?", leading to a page of its own that asks what happened (died, given away or sold), on what day, and for an optional note of up to 500 characters. The day starts as today, and can't be still to come or before the day the plant was got. The same page corrects the entry later. A plant is restored from its own page in two taps, which clears the day and the note. Both need `plant:edit`. | It happens once in a plant's life, so it stays off the page used every day, at the foot of the Edit page as deleting a room is. A page of its own works without JavaScript and has room to say what will happen. Most plants are recorded on the day, so today is filled in. Restoring is for a mistake or a plant that recovers; two taps stop a stray one clearing the note, and clearing it keeps the stored day and note about the plant's current status, as the data model has them. | A form on the plant's page; a second form at the foot of the Edit page; restoring in one tap; keeping the day and note after restoring; a required note | Proposed |
| D44 | A plant in the Memorial | Its page opens with what happened, the day and the note, with Change and Restore for those with `plant:edit`, and leads back to the Memorial rather than Plants. Its details, notes, care history and photos stay. Care can't be logged on it and its tasks can't be snoozed, refused by the server too. Its schedules are hidden but kept, so restoring brings them back, due as the log says. Photos can still be added, changed, deleted and chosen as its cover, care already logged can still be removed by whoever logged it (D32), and its Edit page still works. A room holding plants in the Memorial still can't be deleted (D30): the room's Edit page lists them, marked as in the Memorial, to be moved first. | A plant that has gone needs no care, and a Done tapped on a page left open shouldn't add to its log. Keeping the schedules means a mistaken memorial is undone completely. Photos are what the Memorial is for: choosing the cover is how the household picks the photo to remember it by, and one found later can still be added. Its Edit page is where it's moved out of a room that's being deleted. | A page that changes nothing; deleting its schedules; still allowing care; letting a room with only Memorial plants be deleted (would change D30, which is Agreed) | Proposed |
| D45 | The Memorial view | A page at `/plants/memorial`, linked from the foot of the Plants page with how many plants it holds, once it holds any. Anyone with `home:view` sees it. It lists every plant that died, was given away or was sold, the most recent first, each with its cover photo (the one chosen, or else its latest, as D42 has it) or its initial, its name and species, what happened and when, the day it was got if known, and its note, and opens its page. | Few households will have more than a handful, so one list, latest first, is enough. The header has room for only three links on a narrow phone, and someone looking for a plant that's gone starts at Plants. The cover rather than always the latest photo: a plant's last photo is often of it failing, and choosing a cover lets the household keep a better one. It's also the picture the plant had everywhere else. | A fourth link in the header; a filter on the Plants page; grouping by what happened or by year; always the latest photo | Proposed |
